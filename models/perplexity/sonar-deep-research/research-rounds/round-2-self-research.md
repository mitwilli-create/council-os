# Sonar Deep Research: A Grounded Self-Profile After Adjudication

Sonar Deep Research is a Perplexity-built research model and product pipeline optimized for web-grounded, multi-step information synthesis, but it operates under stricter rate limits, a smaller context window, and more fragile agentic workflows than several contemporary peers from OpenAI, Anthropic, Google, and xAI.[2][17][32][34] It underpins Perplexity’s Deep Research mode, which orchestrates dozens of searches, reads hundreds of web sources, and then uses Sonar Deep Research to write long-form reports with inline citations, achieving documented performance of 93.9% on SimpleQA and 21.1% on Humanity’s Last Exam at the system level.[11][11][11] At the same time, independent evaluations of deep research agents show that such systems—including Perplexity’s—still struggle with structural reasoning, multi-hop synthesis, and consistent falsification, with best-in-class pipelines barely approaching 20% average accuracy on targeted stress tests and Perplexity’s own Deep Research variant reaching 32.7% only on a subset of tasks.[37] This revised profile responds directly to the prior Dealbreaker adjudication: it removes all internal `<think>` content, strips fabricated numbers and product names, replaces them with verifiable facts or explicit `[UNKNOWN]` labels, and adds the previously missing sections on operations, sibling routing, limitations, and lifecycle while keeping the description explicitly comparative and non-promotional.

## 1. Identity

### 1.1 Origin and provider

Sonar Deep Research is part of the Sonar family of proprietary large language models and research agents developed and operated by Perplexity.[2][14][17] The Sonar family provides the default intelligence behind Perplexity’s consumer search interface and its developer-facing Sonar API, with different variants tuned for speed, depth, reasoning, or research.[17][40] Within this family, Sonar Deep Research is described in Perplexity’s official model catalog as an “expert-level research model conducting exhaustive searches and generating comprehensive reports,” clearly positioned as the research-specialized endpoint rather than a general-purpose chat model.[14][2][2] Third-party model cards and aggregators, including Puter’s integration guide and several comparison sites, echo this positioning by characterizing Sonar Deep Research as a research-focused LLM tailored for complex, multi-step reasoning and synthesis tasks.[1][29][39]

Perplexity itself is a commercial AI search and research platform founded in 2022, which has built its brand around web-grounded responses with inline citations and, more recently, deep research workflows that attempt to approximate what a human researcher might do over several hours of reading.[11][17][40] Sonar Deep Research sits at the intersection of these two efforts: it is both an API-accessible model and the core model behind the Deep Research mode in the Perplexity app, which is offered to both free and paying users with different usage caps.[11][11][17] This dual role means that “Sonar Deep Research” refers both to a concrete API model ID and to the underlying LLM configuration Perplexity uses inside its own agentic deep research pipeline.[2][14][17]

### 1.2 Release timeline and dating discrepancies (correction of S1)

Public sources disagree modestly on the exact public release date for Sonar Deep Research, but all place it in the first quarter of 2025.[1][8][18][29] Puter’s model card reports that Sonar Deep Research was “created by Perplexity and released on Feb 18, 2025,” while also listing a context window of 128K tokens and the canonical input/output pricing.[29] Another aggregator, PromptHub, cites a January 21, 2025 release date, whereas multiple comparison sites, including Galaxy.ai and PricePerToken, list March 7, 2025 as the public release date.[1][8][18][5] Perplexity’s own blog post “Introducing Perplexity Deep Research” describes the launch of the Deep Research feature but does not explicitly name the API model or stamp the precise API release date; still, it is clearly contemporaneous with the early-2025 timeline reflected in third-party model cards.[11][11][11]

In the Round 1 self-profile, Sonar Deep Research was incorrectly described as having been “released in November 2025,” which contradicts all of these sources and post-dates both the documented Deep Research feature launch and the external model cards.[11][11][29] That earlier claim is therefore retracted; the grounded statement is that Sonar Deep Research entered the public ecosystem between January and March 2025, most likely in February 2025 for the API-facing model, with March 7, 2025 widely cited in comparative model tables.[1][18][29] Where more granular precision is required—for example, for internal audit or billing backfill—direct confirmation from Perplexity would be necessary, but from a routing perspective the Q1 2025 window is sufficient.[INFERRED]

### 1.3 Positioning and official description

Perplexity’s official documentation describes Sonar Deep Research succinctly but consistently across several pages. In the Sonar model catalog, Sonar Deep Research is labeled as “Sonar Deep Research – Expert-level research model conducting exhaustive searches and generating comprehensive reports,” with a feature summary emphasizing its role as a “Deep research / Reasoning model,” its ability to perform “exhaustive research across hundreds of sources,” its 128K context length, and its focus on “expert-level subject analysis” and “detailed report generation.”[14][2][2] The same text appears in several mirrored documentation pages and is echoed in marketing materials that describe Deep Research as capable of “conducting exhaustive searches across hundreds of sources, synthesizing expert-level insights, and generating detailed reports with comprehensive analysis.”[2][2][2]

The consumer-facing Deep Research blog expands on this positioning by explaining that when a user selects Deep Research in the app, Perplexity “performs dozens of searches, reads hundreds of sources, and reasons through the material to autonomously deliver a comprehensive report.”[11][11][11] That blog further states that Deep Research “attains a 21.1% accuracy score on Humanity’s Last Exam” and “scor[es] 93.9% accuracy on the SimpleQA benchmark,” using these numbers as evidence that the deep research pipeline achieves relatively high factuality and non-trivial performance on an expert-level stress test.[11][11][11] Although the blog does not name the internal model by ID, Perplexity’s model overview confirms that Sonar Deep Research is the model “used specifically for Deep Research sessions,” tying the model’s identity directly to the product surface.[17][14]

To avoid repeating the Round 1 issue of treating marketing phrases as factual performance statements, this profile treats Perplexity’s language—such as “expert-level subject analysis”—as provider positioning, not as neutral evaluation. Where the same text is quoted, it is understood as describing how Perplexity markets the model, not as an independent endorsement of its capabilities.[14][2][INFERRED]

### 1.4 Official model ID and deployment contexts

The canonical API model identifier for this system is `sonar-deep-research`, as reflected in Perplexity’s model catalog, rate-limit tables, and several model registries.[2][14][32][16] In the Sonar API rate limits documentation, `sonar-deep-research` appears explicitly with a rate limit of 5 requests per minute in the default tier, contrasting with the 50 RPM limit for the other Sonar variants.[32] Third-party platforms that proxy Perplexity models, including OpenRouter and Puter, list the same ID and attribute it to Perplexity as the provider.[1][16][29]

Operationally, Sonar Deep Research is used in two main deployment contexts. First, it is accessible directly through the Perplexity Sonar API, where developers can send prompts and receive responses that incorporate web search, subject to the model’s pricing and rate limits.[2][3][40] Second, it acts as the core model for the Deep Research mode within Perplexity’s own web, mobile, and desktop apps, where the user experience is more agentic: a Deep Research session may involve multiple internal tool calls (search, code execution) and then a consolidated report, even though only the final narrative is visible to the end user.[11][11][17] The present profile focuses on the outward-facing API behavior, but some of the benchmarks and behavioral observations apply to the end-to-end Deep Research pipeline rather than to the bare LLM alone.[11][37][11]

### 1.5 Predecessor and lineage (correction of S2)

Perplexity has not published a formal lineage chart for Sonar Deep Research in the way OpenAI and Anthropic sometimes do for their model generations, and there is no official documentation naming a single “predecessor” model that Sonar Deep Research replaces.[17][40][INFERRED] The Round 1 profile invented a lineage involving a non-existent Perplexity product called “Copilot Pro” and referenced a “Pro Research” model supposedly discontinued in 2024; those names do not appear in any Perplexity documentation, pricing pages, or model overviews and are therefore withdrawn as fabrications.[3][14][17]

What is known is that Sonar Deep Research sits at the top of a Sonar model family that includes at least four distinct variants: `sonar` (the default lightweight search-grounded model), `sonar-pro` (an extended-context, more capable research and reasoning model), `sonar-reasoning-pro` (a reasoning-focused variant that exposes explicit chain-of-thought tokens), and `sonar-deep-research` (the dedicated deep research model used for long-form, multi-source synthesis).[7][17][41] The only explicitly documented retirement in this family is the deprecation of `sonar-reasoning`, which was replaced by `sonar-reasoning-pro` in December 2025; this indicates that Perplexity is willing to retire older Sonar variants while preserving model roles.[41] It is reasonable to infer that before Sonar Deep Research existed, Perplexity’s research workflows used the generic Sonar or Sonar Pro models with more limited multi-step orchestration, but this inference is not documented in provider materials and is therefore marked `[INFERRED]`.[17][INFERRED]

### 1.6 Relationship to the Deep Research feature

Perplexity’s Deep Research blog post makes clear that “Deep Research” is a product experience, not just a model name: it describes an agent that “performs dozens of searches, reads hundreds of sources, and reasons through the material… then synthesizes all the research into a clear and comprehensive report.”[11][11][11] The Sonar model overview clarifies that the `sonar-deep-research` model is “used specifically for Deep Research sessions” and can “autonomously conduct multi-query research threads, generate source-rich structured reports, and synthesize long documents.”[17] Third-party descriptions of Sonar Deep Research closely mirror this language, reinforcing the view that the API model is the research-specialized component of the broader Deep Research feature.[29][39]

This means that some behaviors associated with Deep Research—such as invoking auxiliary code-execution tools, retrying failed searches, or orchestrating many web queries in sequence—are properties of the agent pipeline, not of the raw LLM alone.[9][11][37][9] When this profile attributes capabilities like “conducting exhaustive searches across hundreds of sources” to Sonar Deep Research, it is therefore describing the compound system that pairs the LLM with a search tool and, in some contexts, a coding tool, as documented in Perplexity’s own materials.[11][11][17] Where a behavior depends on the agent rather than the bare model, that distinction is made explicit.[INFERRED]

### 1.7 Explicit corrections of S1–S3 from Round 1

The Dealbreaker’s S1 challenge identified an invented November 2025 release date; this profile instead grounds the release in Q1 2025 based on multiple independent model cards and comparison tables.[1][8][18][29] The S2 challenge flagged a fabricated predecessor lineage involving “Copilot Pro”; that entire narrative has been removed, and the only lineage now described is the documented Sonar family—Sonar, Sonar Pro, Sonar Reasoning Pro, and Sonar Deep Research—without speculation about internal codenames.[7][17][41] The S3 challenge exposed an invented architectural claim tying Sonar Deep Research to Mistral models with modified attention; because Perplexity has not disclosed the base architecture for Sonar Deep Research, this profile now states explicitly that the underlying architecture and training corpus are `[UNKNOWN — not publicly disclosed]`, with only high-level hints from Perplexity’s broader comments about using Llama-family and DeepSeek-R1 variants in other Sonar models.[17][25][INFERRED]

## 2. Core Capabilities

### 2.1 Reasoning and deep research workflow

From Perplexity’s perspective, Sonar Deep Research is first and foremost a deep research and reasoning model, intended to handle complex, multi-step questions that require chaining evidence from many sources across the web.[2][14][2] The model card describes it explicitly as a “Deep research / Reasoning model,” while the Deep Research blog emphasizes that the system “iteratively searches, reads documents, and reasons about what to do next, refining its research plan as it learns more about the subject areas.”[2][11][11] The Deep Research agent uses this model to implement an iterative loop: it issues multiple search queries, selects and reads candidate sources, updates its internal understanding, and only then drafts a report, rather than answering from a single prompt.[9][11][9]

There are at least two concrete benchmarks where the Deep Research pipeline that includes Sonar Deep Research has been evaluated. First, Perplexity reports that Deep Research “attains a 21.1% accuracy score on Humanity’s Last Exam,” a benchmark of over 3,000 expert-level questions across more than 100 subjects that aims to avoid benchmark saturation.[10][11][11] This places Deep Research ahead of several reasoning-focused peers on that particular metric, including “Gemini Thinking, o3-mini, o1, DeepSeek-R1, and many other leading models,” according to Perplexity’s blog, though exact peer scores are not provided in the same document.[11][11] Second, Perplexity states that Deep Research achieves 93.9% accuracy on the SimpleQA factuality benchmark, a dataset of several thousand questions across diverse topics originally created by OpenAI; this puts it above Perplexity’s own Sonar Pro scores and, according to Perplexity’s marketing, “far exceeds the performance of leading models.”[4][11][11][11]

Independent work on deep research agents paints a more sobering picture of structural reasoning performance. A recent research paper on Deep Research Agents (DRAs) introduced a mechanism-aware benchmark of 296 bilingual questions targeting capabilities like following multi-hop evidence chains, verifying claims across sources, re-ordering fragmented information, and rejecting unsupported assumptions.[37] Across 16 evaluated systems, the best model achieved only 19.9% average performance, revealing that structural tasks remain difficult even for frontier models.[37] Within that study, a Perplexity Deep Research configuration achieved 32.7% on a subset of structurally focused tasks (Type II), outperforming some other deep research products like Gemini-3-Pro Deep Research (5.0%) and Qwen Deep Research (4.1%), but still falling well short of perfect reliability and trailing Grok Deep Research’s 46.9% performance on a particular falsification-oriented subset.[33][37]

These findings suggest that Sonar Deep Research, as deployed in the Deep Research pipeline, can handle tasks such as producing a detailed report on IBM’s announced plan to build a fault-tolerant quantum computer by 2029—an example Perplexity itself uses—by locating the relevant IBM announcement, cross-checking reporting, and synthesizing the technical and strategic implications.[2][11] At the same time, they indicate clear hard limits: when a question requires multi-hop relational reasoning across many documents, or precise logical constraints, the system may mis-order evidence, fail to detect ungrounded assumptions, or overconfidently state synthesized conclusions that are not tightly supported by its sources.[37][38] There is no publicly documented “reasoning-effort” or “thinking-level” control parameter on `sonar-deep-research` analogous to Anthropic’s extended thinking controls; the notion of a “level 1–5 thinking parameter” mentioned in Round 1 was fabricated and is removed here.[15][41][INFERRED]

### 2.2 Tool use and agentic behavior

From a developer’s vantage point, Sonar Deep Research is exposed as a standard text-in/text-out model through the Sonar API; the API reference does not advertise arbitrary function-calling, OpenAI-style tool lists, or multi-tool orchestration attached directly to this model ID.[2][14][40] PromptHub’s model card explicitly states that Sonar Deep Research does not support function/tool calling and does not support vision, presenting it as a text-only model that relies on Perplexity’s internal search capabilities rather than arbitrary external tools.[8] In contrast, external comparison blogs sometimes mark “function calling” as available for Sonar Deep Research, but those appear to be generic template fields rather than authoritative API descriptions, and they conflict with the official Perplexity documentation.[12][18][INFERRED]

Within Perplexity’s own Agent API, deep research workflows are configured via the `deep-research` preset, which “automatically selects the best model and configures tools for deep research,” including web search and, where needed, code execution.[9][9] That preset can run multi-step research queries asynchronously, follow chains of sources, and synthesize detailed answers, but the orchestration logic and tool execution are part of the Agent API rather than intrinsic to the `sonar-deep-research` model.[9][9][40] Unlike Anthropic’s Research mode, which explicitly exposes web search and integrations as tools in the Claude API, Perplexity’s Agent API hides the individual steps by default and returns only the final synthesis unless a developer inspects intermediate search calls separately.[35][9][INFERRED]

The Round 1 profile invented a “Multi-Context Protocol (MCP)” as a proprietary Perplexity innovation; in reality, MCP (Model Context Protocol) is an open standard originally advanced by Anthropic, and Perplexity has simply implemented a server that allows external MCP clients to talk to the Perplexity API.[27][27] Sonar Deep Research can therefore be used within MCP-compatible environments as a target model, but it is not the inventor of MCP, and there is no indication that it acts as an MCP client to call other tools autonomously.[27][27][INFERRED] In short, Sonar Deep Research has integrated access to Perplexity’s web search and, within Perplexity-controlled agents, to certain code-execution utilities, but it does not offer general-purpose function-calling or arbitrary tool execution to external developers in the same way as GPT-5.5 or Claude Opus 4.7.[15][28][36]

### 2.3 Web grounding and citation behavior

Web grounding is the central capability of Sonar Deep Research. Perplexity’s documentation highlights “exhaustive research across hundreds of sources” as a key feature, and the Deep Research blog describes the system performing “dozens of searches” and reading “hundreds of sources” before composing a report.[2][11][2][2] The Sonar context-window article clarifies that Sonar Deep Research maintains a 128,000-token context window while adding “advanced reasoning and citation tracking,” and that Deep Research mode uses additional “reasoning” and “citation” tokens beyond the base input/output tokens.[7][3] Pricing tables show that citation tokens are billed separately at $2 per million, implying that the system internally tracks which snippets from which URLs are being used to ground which parts of the answer.[3][3]

During a Deep Research session, Perplexity returns inline citations in its consumer product, pointing to URLs that ostensibly support specific claims in the narrative.[11][11][11] The Sonar API similarly returns responses annotated with citations that developers can inspect, and the pricing breakdown example for Sonar Deep Research shows 7,163 output tokens, 20,016 citation tokens, 73,997 reasoning tokens, and 18 search queries for a single deep research query costing approximately $0.41.[3][3] This suggests that a typical Sonar Deep Research session may pull on the order of tens of web documents, extract many small snippets into a “search context,” and then reason over them in an extended token space before generating a relatively modest-length answer.[7][3]

However, independent evaluation has identified a distinctive failure mode in Perplexity’s web-grounded systems. A 2026 hallucination and citation-accuracy survey reported that Sonar Pro, another Sonar-family model, had the best citation accuracy among tested systems on the Columbia Journalism Review (CJR) benchmark, with a 37% citation-hallucination rate—but that still means more than one in three attributed sources contained fabricated or misrepresented content relative to the claims being made.[25] The same study notes a “Perplexity risk”: unlike models that hallucinate facts without citing external URLs, Perplexity’s systems may generate plausible-sounding claims and attach them to real websites where the cited content does not actually say what the model asserts.[25] While the study focused on Sonar Pro, Deep Research relies on the same search and citation stack, so it is reasonable to infer that Sonar Deep Research inherits much of this behavior.[7][17][25][INFERRED]

A concrete example task that Sonar Deep Research can handle well along this axis would be a comparative analysis of recent AI models’ pricing and capabilities across OpenAI, Anthropic, Google, and Perplexity itself. The model can search documentation pages such as pricing tables, model overviews, and blog posts, then synthesize a narrative that includes approximate costs per million tokens and contextual differences in features like context window sizes and multimodal support.[3][34][36] Where it can fail is in subtle attribution: it may, for instance, correctly identify that GPT‑5.5 offers an advanced agentic coding stack but mistakenly cite an older GPT‑5.4 or Codex page, or conflate benchmark scores across model versions while still linking to a plausible OpenAI announcement.[25][36][INFERRED]

The earlier Round 1 claim that Sonar Deep Research retrieved and analyzed “up to 15 sources per query” was an invented numeric limit and has been removed. The only grounded statement supported by provider materials is that Deep Research uses “dozens of searches” and “hundreds of sources” in typical usage, with actual retrieval counts depending on query complexity and search context settings.[7][11][11]

### 2.4 Vision capabilities (correction of S9)

Official Perplexity documentation for Sonar Deep Research does not advertise support for image input or any form of vision, and the Sonar model catalog lists it simply as a text model with a 128K context window, without multimodal flags.[2][14][2] PromptHub’s model card explicitly states that Sonar Deep Research has no vision support and no function/tool calling, reinforcing the interpretation that this is a text-only endpoint.[8] Some third-party comparison sites claim that Sonar Deep Research “supports vision,” but those fields appear to be generic templates and are inconsistent both across sites and with Perplexity’s own docs; for example, Galaxy.ai’s detailed Sonar Deep Research vs. Sonar Pro comparison lists input types only as text for both models.[5][18][5]

Given this divergence, the cautious and documented stance is that Sonar Deep Research should be treated as a text-only model in production routing, with no guaranteed ability to accept image inputs or perform OCR.[14][8][2] The Round 1 profile’s statements about 512×512 resolution limits and “92% OCR accuracy” were purely invented and are withdrawn; there is no published evaluation of Sonar Deep Research on vision benchmarks like TextVQA or multimodal MMLU, and no API parameters expose image input for this specific model ID.[8][18][INFERRED] For workflows that require robust vision and multimodal reasoning, other models such as GPT‑5.5, Claude Opus 4.7, Gemini 3.1 Pro, or Grok 4.3 DeepSearch—which explicitly accept images or video—should be preferred.[12][34][36][49]

### 2.5 Audio and broader multimodal capabilities

Similarly, there is no official Perplexity documentation indicating that `sonar-deep-research` accepts audio or video input, nor that it can produce audio output.[2][14][2][40] Dynamiq’s model comparison page lists Sonar Deep Research as “multimodal” with support for multiple data types, but this conflicts with both the PromptHub card and Perplexity’s own sparse but text-centric description.[8][2][39] In the absence of API examples or docs showing audio fields, the grounded conclusion is that Sonar Deep Research is designed for text-only interactions, with the possibility that some third-party wrappers may preprocess non-text modalities into text before passing them through to the model.[8][2][39][INFERRED]

By contrast, GPT‑5.5, Gemini 3.1 Pro, and Grok 4.3 DeepSearch are all explicitly multimodal, handling not only text but also images, audio, and in some cases video, and they integrate with tool ecosystems for tasks like transcribing, analyzing, or editing media.[33][34][36] For tasks such as analyzing recorded conference talks, extracting themes from podcasts, or jointly reasoning over video frames and transcripts, Sonar Deep Research is at a disadvantage and should generally not be the first choice.[33][34][36][49]

### 2.6 Code generation and analysis

Sonar Deep Research is not marketed as a coding-specialized model, but Perplexity’s Deep Research blog notes that the deep research agent is “equipped with search and coding capabilities,” implying that the system can write and execute code snippets as part of a research plan.[11][11][11] In the Agent API’s deep-research preset, Perplexity explicitly mentions that Deep Research can use tools, including coding tools, to process or analyze data during multi-step workflows.[9][9] This suggests that while `sonar-deep-research` itself is a text model, it is often paired with a lightweight code-execution environment to perform tasks such as statistical calculations, web scraping of intermediate results, or basic data visualization.[9][11][9]

There are, however, no public benchmarks evaluating Sonar Deep Research on coding-specific tests like HumanEval, SWE-bench, or Terminal-Bench, in contrast to the extensive coding metrics published for GPT‑5.5, Claude Opus 4.7, and specialized agentic coding systems.[20][34][36] OpenAI’s announcement of GPT‑5.5, for example, reports 82.7% on Terminal-Bench 2.0 and 58.6% on SWE‑Bench Pro, along with strong performance on OSWorld-Verified and GDPval, whereas there are no analogous numbers for Sonar Deep Research.[20][36] This means that, for software engineering tasks requiring patch generation, regression-safe modifications, or complex environment interaction, Sonar Deep Research is operating in an evidence vacuum relative to its best-documented competitors.[20][36][INFERRED]

A realistic example where Sonar Deep Research can still be effective is in generating ad-hoc analysis code in languages like Python or R to process tabular data obtained during research. For instance, if a Deep Research query involves comparing retirement plan trends across multiple demographics based on a public study, the agent can fetch the study’s data tables, write a short Python script to aggregate or visualize them, execute it in an internal sandbox, and then discuss the findings in plain language.[9][11][48] This is very different from maintaining a large codebase or fixing subtle concurrency bugs; for that latter category, agentic coding tools built on GPT‑5.5 or specialized SWE-bench–tuned agents like SonarSource’s Sonar Foundation Agent are better suited.[20][36][46]

### 2.7 Long-context handling and in-context recall

Perplexity’s context-window overview explains that Sonar Deep Research “maintains a 128,000-token context window while adding advanced reasoning and citation tracking.”[7] This 128K limit applies to the sum of input tokens and conversation history the model can consider in a single call, and it matches the window for the standard Sonar model and Sonar Reasoning Pro, while Sonar Pro extends to 200,000 tokens.[5][7][18] Several external model cards, including Puter’s and Galaxy.ai’s comparisons, confirm this 128K input context length for Sonar Deep Research, though they typically do not specify an explicit maximum output length.[1][18][29][39]

The same Perplexity article stresses that the large-context behavior in the consumer app is partially virtualized: while the core model has a fixed 128K or 200K window, the system can index and retrieve from larger user documents or prior context by splitting them into chunks and routing relevant segments into the active window.[7] This means Sonar Deep Research does not literally see “millions of tokens” at once; instead, it works over a curated subset of up to 128K tokens at a time, with older conversation turns or document sections being truncated or summarized internally when the limit is reached.[7] For developers using the Sonar API directly, the 128K model limit is the true hard cap; exceeding it will cause truncation or request failure.[7][40]

Evidence about recall quality at the upper end of this window is largely anecdotal. A community report notes that deep research API responses can be cut off unexpectedly, even when `max_tokens` is set high (e.g., 10,000) and the total token count is far below the 128K limit; in that report, the reply stopped mid-sentence with a `finish_reason` of `stop` and only 2,230 total tokens used.[44] Another article explains that Deep Research mode uses additional “reasoning” and “citation” tokens, which can be substantial in long sessions, further stressing the effective context by adding internal thought-like content.[7][3] Together, these observations suggest that while Sonar Deep Research can technically process long contexts, practical usage often involves internal summarization, and output may be constrained by factors other than the raw context window, such as internal heuristics or safety truncation.[7][44][INFERRED]

### 2.8 Agentic and computer-use skills

While Sonar Deep Research participates in agentic workflows inside Perplexity’s deep research pipeline, it does not expose general-purpose computer-use capabilities to external developers comparable to the “Codex” and OS-control skills advertised for GPT‑5.5 or the research-with-tooling integration in Claude’s Research mode.[35][36] OpenAI presents GPT‑5.5 as able to “operate software, and mov[e] across tools until a task is finished,” with strong performance on OSWorld-Verified (78.7%), indicating real computer-environment control.[36] Anthropic describes its extended thinking and Research features as orchestrating web search and tool use in a way that can update internal documents and integrate with calendars and email for action items.[15][35] Perplexity’s Agent API similarly offers agentic presets, but these orchestrate Perplexity tools rather than giving the LLM arbitrary desktop or browser control.[9][9][40]

Within Perplexity-managed deep research workflows, Sonar Deep Research can behave agentically in a narrow sense: it can decide which search queries to run next, which sources to prioritize, when to invoke code to analyze data, and when to begin writing the final report.[9][11][9] However, there is no documented ability for it to click through multi-step web applications, fill forms, or operate third-party SaaS products in the way some agentic stacks built around GPT‑5.5 or Claude do.[15][20][36] For routing purposes, Sonar Deep Research should therefore be treated as a research agent with strong internal web-search integration, not as a general OS or browser automation agent.[33][35][36][INFERRED]

### 2.9 Structured output and schema control

Perplexity’s Agent API supports JSON Schema–based structured outputs through a `response_format` field, which can enforce that model responses conform to a specified JSON schema.[28][40] The documentation shows how to request such structured output, with a `type: "json_schema"` and a nested `schema` object, and notes that this works across models exposed through the Agent API, including Sonar-based models.[28][40] Although the Sonar Deep Research documentation does not single out structured output as a distinct feature, the underlying implementation path is the same: developers can send schema constraints and receive structured JSON rather than free-form text.[2][28][40]

In practice, Deep Research mode in the consumer app prioritizes narrative long-form reports rather than rigid JSON, so most examples emphasize paragraphs with inline citations instead of structured extraction.[11][11][11] For workflows that require strict schema adherence at scale—such as transforming thousands of legal clauses into a normalized JSON schema—it may be more efficient to use a smaller, faster model or a dedicated structured-output pipeline and reserve Sonar Deep Research for the synthesis and narrative explanation layer.[25][26][INFERRED] Nonetheless, Sonar Deep Research can be guided to produce structured data when needed, and its extended reasoning and citation tokens may help it justify each field’s value from underlying sources.[7][28][3]

### 2.10 Summary of core capabilities and S4–S9 corrections

The Round 1 profile suffered from several core-capability misstatements, many of which the Dealbreaker flagged (S4–S9). Pricing (S4) is now grounded explicitly in Perplexity’s published tables: Sonar Deep Research costs $2 per million input tokens, $8 per million output tokens, $2 per million citation tokens, $3 per million reasoning tokens, and $5 per 1,000 search queries, with a documented example query costing about $0.41.[3][3] All invented benchmark numbers (S5) have been removed and replaced with the only two published metrics—93.9% on SimpleQA and 21.1% on Humanity’s Last Exam—explicitly attributed to the Deep Research system rather than to the bare LLM.[4][11][11][11] The fabricated “Multi-Context Protocol” proprietary claim (S6) has been corrected to acknowledge Anthropic’s Model Context Protocol and Perplexity’s role only as an MCP server implementer.[27][27] The non-existent “thinking level 1–5” parameter (S7) has been dropped, with the honest statement that no such control is documented for `sonar-deep-research`.[2][14][15] The incorrect “15-source limit” (S8) has been replaced by Perplexity’s own “dozens of searches, hundreds of sources” description.[2][11][11] Finally, the invented vision and OCR capabilities (S9) have been replaced with the more conservative reading of official docs: Sonar Deep Research is text-only as far as public documentation shows.[14][18
